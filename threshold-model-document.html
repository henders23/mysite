The Threshold Model

A design framework for rigorous, heavily personalised higher education, with comparative course designs for Engineering, Law and English for Academic Purposes.

Please note. These are suggestions and directions for exploration, not settled answers. They draw on published research and practice but have not been peer reviewed. Adapt what you use to your context, and check your institution’s policies before acting on it.

Who it's for What's inside References A note on the evidence

Who it's for

  • Programme teams designing or redesigning a course
  • Assessment leads setting policy on AI and judgement
  • Academic developers supporting staff through the change

What's inside

Ten parts and four appendices.

  1. Part 1Why a new model is neededFour failures it fixes: feedback scarcity, judgement conflation, the authenticity gap, and standardisation mistaken for rigour.
  2. Part 2Ten design principlesFrom signed human judgement to designed delight.
  3. Part 3The architectureThe Workshop, Commons and Bench; the Threshold and the Return; the Spine, Provocation and the Warrant.
  4. Part 4The feedback ladderVerdict, interrogation, deliberation and judgement, and how to configure AI to question rather than answer.
  5. Part 5The assessment triadArtefact, account and answering, and making the live conversation affordable.
  6. Part 6The teaching rhythmA generic twelve-week pattern.
  7. Part 7Three disciplines, three instantiationsEngineering, Law and EAP compared, with course design and integrity tables.
  8. Part 8What changes for academic staffThree new competences, and an honest account of workload.
  9. Part 9Institutional conditionsSix conditions for the model to hold, and nine risks with mitigations.
  10. Part 10Evaluating the modelFalsifiable claims, indicators, and a three-year implementation.

Appendices

  • AAI-use declaration template
  • BQuestion architecture for the live conversation
  • CWorkshop prompt patterns, with Engineering, Law and EAP versions
  • DOne-page summary

A note on the evidence

The list is indicative rather than exhaustive. It was checked against publisher and library records in November 2026, and two entries were corrected: the full author list for Tai et al. (2023), and page numbers and a DOI for Perkins et al. (2024).

The model's claims about workload and effect are design claims, set out in the document as falsifiable propositions with indicators for evaluation. They have not yet been tested in a full implementation.
