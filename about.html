Paul Hendrie

I have taught in higher education for over 17 years, specialising in Applied Linguistics and academic communication within STEM disciplines. I have years of experience in designing practitioner development courses for expert educators, which has given me strong grounding in the science of learning.

I take a pragmatic yet principled view of AI. It is here, it is disruptive, and it demands a response. Approaches to assessment and teaching must be 'AI resilient' to minimise the risk of student misuse, but these tools can also be used to personalise learning, to differentiate between students, and add greater depth to the learning process.

AI education offerings are often 'bitty', unsystematic, overly technical, and lacking clear use cases. I show educators the value of these tools as they relate to learning, and equip practitioners with the skills to use them — and to know when not to.

My work with AI tools sits at the intersection of learning design, educational technology, and professional development. I've collaborated with educators from STEM, humanities, health sciences, and the creative arts, and the common thread with AI is often the same: how do we respond to the challenges presented by AI, and how can we use it to improve learning outcomes?

Guiding principles

Four principles keep the focus on students' experience and outcomes.

  • Student voice firstAI use must extend and promote student perspectives, not silence them.
  • Human-centredAI use must amplify human connection and creativity, underpinned by principles from the literature on learning.
  • Autonomy-buildingAI use must scaffold towards student independence and critical thinking, not dependence.
  • Ethically groundedData protection and academic integrity are foundational to everything I do.
