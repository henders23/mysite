Please note. These are suggestions and directions for exploration, not settled answers. They draw on published research and practice but have not been peer reviewed. Adapt what you use to your context, and check your institution’s policies before acting on it.

Who it's for What's inside References A note on the evidence

Who it's for

Scholarly, but written in clear and accessible language, with a thesis built from the evidence.

  • Teachers setting and marking written work in any discipline
  • Course and programme designers deciding what to assess and how
  • EAP and academic literacies practitioners working with disciplines
  • Anyone writing or revising an institutional AI or integrity policy

What's inside

Ten sections, from theory to practice.

  1. 01IntroductionWhy the sector’s difficulty with AI and authorship is conceptual before it is technological.
  2. 02What do we mean by authorship?The author was never alone: authorship as identity and practice, postplagiarism and the black box.
  3. 03The EAP precedentProofreading, patchwriting and source use: the field has faced this question before.
  4. 04What students are doing and thinkingSurvey evidence on use, confidence and who is using AI most.
  5. 05How institutions are respondingPublishing’s accountability standard, UK and international policy, the detection dead-end and disclosure frameworks.
  6. 06Designing for authorshipSecurity, validity and structure; evaluative judgement; equity risks; what in-sessional provision brings.
  7. 07Across the disciplinesNine genres, from the laboratory report to the creative portfolio.
  8. 08A definition and eight principlesThe working definition, and what it means for teachers and course designers.
  9. 09What would change this positionThree evidence thresholds that would justify a rethink.
  10. 10ConclusionAuthorship is accountability: the author is the one who can answer for the work.

A note on the evidence

Bibliographic details were checked against publisher and official records in September 2026, with remaining items confirmed in November 2026. A few sources need a reader's care:

  • Preprints. The EAP-AIAS (Roe, Perkins and Tregubova, 2024) is a preprint. The AI Assessment Scale revision (Perkins, Roe and Furze, 2024) has since been published in the Journal of University Teaching & Learning Practice, 22(7), 2025.
  • Practice papers. The Sydney two-lane document (Bridgeman, Liu and Weeks, 2024) is institutional guidance, not peer-reviewed research, and is cited as such.
  • Living documents. The ICMJE recommendations are updated periodically. The paper cites the 2025 edition.
