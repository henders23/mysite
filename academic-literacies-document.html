A disciplinary framework and resource for in-sessional EAP provision in a UK university.

Please note. These are suggestions and directions for exploration, not settled answers. They draw on published research and practice but have not been peer reviewed. Adapt what you use to your context, and check your institution’s policies before acting on it.

Who it's for What's inside References A note on the evidence

Who it's for

A working document for in-sessional academic literacies teams: what provision is for, what it should now do, and what it looks like subject by subject.

  • In-sessional EAP and academic literacies practitioners
  • Unit and programme leads planning provision and staff development
  • Discipline staff working in partnership with academic literacies teams

What's inside

Four parts and four appendices.

  1. Part 1The argumentLea and Street's three models applied to AI, what academic literacies knows that AI frameworks don't, the equity case, the practitioner's position, and a seven-dimension construct.
  2. Part 2RedesignThe text-analysis protocol, ten materials-design principles, fourteen in-sessional staples rebuilt, a three-tier delivery model, disclosure and assessment, and working with disciplines.
  3. Part 3The disciplinesThirteen clusters, each with its epistemology, genre families, model failure modes and activities, plus a cross-disciplinary map.
  4. Part 4The practitioner, the unit and the institutionMapping to the BALEAP competency framework, a six-session CPD programme, what to stop doing, evaluation, a research agenda, and how the argument might be wrong.

Appendices

  • AComparative text-analysis proforma
  • BA disclosure statement worth reading
  • CA one-page brief for departmental conversations
  • DActivity index by academic literacies dimension

A note on the evidence

Every reference was checked against Crossref, publisher pages, library catalogues or the issuing body in November 2026. All 117 sources exist. Most corrections were minor; three were substantive:

  • The EAP-AIAS is by Roe, Perkins and Tregubova, published in TESOL Journal in 2026.
  • Corbin et al. (2025) carries its published title, “Where’s the line? It’s an absurd line”.
  • Perkins et al. (2024) carries its published title, beginning “The Artificial Intelligence Assessment Scale”.

Sector reports, policy documents, legislation and one blog post are included as such, not as research. Only the survey data, the detector studies and the regulation are treated as evidence; the rest is argument. The claims about model behaviour should be re-tested every year.
