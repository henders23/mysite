A. Foundations for every discipline

Patterns common to every subject: how outputs are produced, fidelity to sources, learning with a tool rather than through it, abstaining, delegating safely and disclosing.

AI literacy activities

Level, time, tools, data risk and steps are in the full activity list.

  • 1. Mapping your own AI useNoticing how, when and why you use AI: the baseline for later judgements.
  • 2. How was this answer produced?Telling prediction, search, code execution and deep research apart in an output.
  • 3. Does the source say that?Claim–source fidelity: does a cited source exist and support its claim?
  • 4. Ask again, then push backVariability between runs, and sycophancy: agreeing with the user.
  • 5. A tutor, not an answer machineLearning with AI without letting it do the thinking.
  • 6. The line between editing and ghost-writingUsing AI feedback without surrendering authorship or learning.
  • 7. Doing it the hard way: deliberate abstentionChoosing not to use AI, and observing the effect on learning.
  • 8. Delegating to an agentWhat changes when AI acts on a user’s behalf, and who is accountable.
  • 9. Reliance, reassurance and wellbeingEmotional reliance, flattering agreement and anxiety about integrity accusations.
  • 10. Writing an honest disclosure statementDisclosing AI use accurately and proportionately.
