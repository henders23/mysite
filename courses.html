AI-Ready Educator

Confident, critical and creative AI use in HE

Format
Online, 2 × half day
When
Fridays, 9.30–12.30 UK time. Up to 20 staff
Price
On enquiry

Charged per team, up to 20 staff, not per person. Available as 2 × half day or a single half day. Founding-institution rates for the first bookings this year.

A practical working session for programme teams who want to use AI well, without losing sight of learning, integrity or human judgement. Delivered online for your team, using your own modules and assessment briefs.

Two half-days a few weeks apart, so colleagues can test ideas with students in between and come back with real observations. Expect live demos, hands-on tasks and usable outputs, not a briefing.

You finish with
  • A shared position on AI across the team
  • A practical design framework
  • At least one usable artefact or process applied to your own modules
What the sessions cover
  1. Session 1Understanding AI, learning and good designClarify your position on AI in HE: what it should and shouldn’t do. Live examples and guided activities on teaching design, resources, feedback and activity development, with educational purpose at the centre.
  2. Session 2Assessment, integrity and practical creationReconvene after testing ideas in your teaching. Examine misuse, over-reliance and unclear guidance, stress-test your assessment tasks, and build or adapt a usable teaching artefact.

Vibe Coding for Educational Purposes

Build interactive learning tools without writing a line of code

Format
Online, 2 × half day
When
Fridays, 9.30–12.30 UK time. Up to 20 staff
Price
On enquiry

Charged per team, up to 20 staff, not per person. Available as 2 × half day or a single half day. Founding-institution rates for the first bookings this year.

A hands-on, beginner-friendly session for teams who want to build their own digital learning tools — quizzes, interactive activities, simulations, revision aids — with AI as their coding partner. No coding experience needed.

Your team learns to describe what it wants, let AI write the code, and stay in control of the result. Between sessions colleagues build for their own modules; session two reviews what they made, so the skills stick.

You finish with
  • Working, publishable tools the team built themselves
  • Tools published with a link or embedded in your VLE
  • A repeatable workflow for making more
What the sessions cover
  1. Session 1Coding with AI as your partnerWhat vibe coding actually means, the right tools for educators, prompts that produce working results, and your first small interactive activity by lunchtime — plus how to read and refine what the AI produces.
  2. Session 2Publishing, embedding and reviewReview what the team built, troubleshoot together, then publish each tool with a public link or embed it in your VLE or course page.

Module & Assessment Redesign Lab

The workshops build capability. The Lab produces documents.

Format
Online, 3 × half day over 6 weeks
When
Fridays. Capped at 12 staff
Price
On enquiry

Charged per programme team, up to 12 staff. Includes redesign of up to six assessment briefs, with one round of written feedback per draft. Founding-institution rates available.

Bring one module or programme and leave with assessment that is redesigned, written up and ready to put in front of an exam board.

Three half-day sessions across six weeks, with drafting in between and written feedback from me on every draft. Built on the Assessment redesign map.

You finish with
  • Redesigned assessment briefs for the module
  • Marking criteria aligned to the new briefs
  • A student-facing AI-use statement
  • Short staff guidance for whoever teaches it next
How the six weeks run
  1. Week 1TriageAudit every assessment for AI vulnerability and sort it: compromised, defensible with adjustment, or fine as it stands. You leave knowing which briefs need rewriting and why.
  2. Weeks 2–3DraftingThe team drafts revised briefs; I return written feedback on each before we meet.
  3. Week 4RedesignRebuild the briefs with authentic-assessment patterns that are AI-resilient by design, align criteria, and draft student guidance on acceptable AI use.
  4. Weeks 5–6Refine, finalise and pressure-testA further round of feedback, then test the package against what an exam board and an integrity panel will ask, and finalise it as a set the programme can adopt.

The AI Collegium

Teaching, learning and AI in higher education

Format
Online cohort, four phases
When
January to June 2027. Limited places
Price
£499 per participant

Places are limited to keep the cohort dialogue deep. Register your interest and I’ll send full details.

A semester-long community of colleagues who learn and think together, developing solutions to learning design challenges in their own context.

Over five months you work through curated resources, artefacts, readings and research, with space to develop responses to what is playing out in your own teaching. Rich peer dialogue, generous expert input, and tangible outputs.

You finish with
  • A portfolio of resources for your own practice
  • A network of peers across institutions
  • The confidence to lead AI-informed change where you work
The four phases
  1. Jan–Feb 2027Designing for learningA grounding in the science of learning — Bloom’s taxonomy, cognitive load, constructive alignment — before touching a single AI tool.
  2. Mar–Apr 2027Building with AIAI-informed activities, interactive artefacts, AI literacy activities, multimodal resources and simple software applications.
  3. Apr–May 2027Assessment and feedbackThe contested territory: authentic assessment and academic integrity in the new landscape.
  4. May–Jun 2027Leading changeTurn what you’ve learned into coherent practice, and plan how to support colleagues in your institution.

For faculties and institutions

Consultancy

The workshops serve a team. The Lab serves a programme. Consultancy is for the work that crosses both: strategy, policy and staff development at faculty or institutional scale, designed around your context rather than a fixed format.

  • Staff development programmesMulti-department or institution-wide AI capability programmes, sequenced and coherent, built so your own educational developers can sustain them after I step back.
  • Assessment strategyA faculty-level review of where AI has really compromised integrity, where it hasn't, and what a defensible response looks like in practice.
  • Curriculum and graduate attributesMapping where and how generative AI should be embedded across programmes, and what students should be able to do with it by graduation.
  • Policy and academic integrityAI guidelines, acceptable-use policies and integrity frameworks written to hold up at a misconduct panel and survive the next model release.
